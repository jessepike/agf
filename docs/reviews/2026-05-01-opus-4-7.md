## Findings

### O47-F01 — "Composition Patterns" is overloaded; identical name, two different referents

- **ID:** O47-F01
- **Title:** "Composition Patterns" names two distinct construct sets in canonical docs
- **Severity:** High
- **Confidence in your own finding:** Established — exact citations below
- **Dimension:** Coherence (and Clarity)
- **Where:** `docs/shared-vocabulary.md:287–296` ("Four progressive governance architectures … Canonical count: four") vs. `docs/agentic-primitives.md:1289–1316` ("### Composition Patterns" under "## Multi-Agent Coordination" — Sequential / Parallel / Orchestrated)
- **What the framework claims:** D10(a) canonicalized **the** Composition Patterns at four (MVC / Validation Pipeline / GDF / Full Governed). Independently, agentic-primitives.md uses the same heading "Composition Patterns" for three multi-agent topology patterns.
- **What's wrong:** A reader who has internalized D10's vocabulary lands on `agentic-primitives.md:1289` and finds three patterns called the same thing. This is exactly the failure mode D10b/D10c were created to eliminate for "governance" and "observability" — and it has been left in place for "Composition Patterns" itself, the term D10 elevated.
- **Recommended fix:** Rename the multi-agent section to **"Multi-Agent Topology Patterns"** (or "Coordination Topologies") in `agentic-primitives.md:1289`, mirror the change in `shared-vocabulary.md`, add a sense-disambiguation row to the existing Composition Patterns vocabulary entry, and add a one-line cross-reference at both call sites.

### O47-F02 — REVISE(context) invalidates an approval, but the GDR lifecycle has no transition for it

- **ID:** O47-F02
- **Title:** Promised GDR emission for stale-approval invalidation has no matching lifecycle state
- **Severity:** High
- **Confidence:** Established — three-doc citation
- **Dimension:** Mechanical Integrity (and Coherence)
- **Where:** `docs/agentic-primitives.md:1406` (per-signal table, REVISE(context) row: "stale-approval invalidation **may emit a GDR** for the invalidated approval (see Approval Expiration below)"); `docs/agentic-primitives.md:1437–1439` (Approval Expiration says "the approval expires"); `docs/governance-decision-record.md:58, 70` (`expired` is defined as `valid_until` having elapsed; no other trigger is admitted in the Lifecycle Invariants table)
- **What the framework claims:** A GDR may be emitted for an approval invalidated by REVISE(context), and the path goes through Approval Expiration.
- **What's wrong:** GDR lifecycle defines `expired` strictly by clock (`valid_until` elapsed). REVISE(context)-triggered invalidation is *event-driven*, not clock-driven. There is no defined transition for "previously `resolved` GDR invalidated by upstream context change." Three coherent options exist (`expired` widened, `superseded` by a re-resolution GDR, or a new `invalidated` state) and the framework picks none. An implementer cannot serialize the very GDR the per-signal table promises.
- **Recommended fix:** Either (a) widen `expired` semantics to include "context-invalidation by REVISE(context)" with a required `expired_reason` field; or (b) require Ring 2 to emit a successor GDR (`status: resolved`, `gate_resolution: REVOKED` — adding a sixth Gate Resolution would be a vocabulary cost) and mark the prior as `superseded`; or (c) add a sixth lifecycle status `invalidated` covering this case. Pick one; update lifecycle invariants table; update per-signal table to point to the chosen transition rather than the under-specified Approval Expiration paragraph.

### O47-F03 — DELEGATE and ERROR(degrade) GDR emission is non-local to the issuer

- **ID:** O47-F03
- **Title:** Whether DELEGATE / ERROR(degrade) emit a GDR depends on the receiving Ring 2's policy, not the issuing ring's state
- **Severity:** Medium
- **Confidence:** Established — per-signal table rows quote the conditional
- **Dimension:** Coherence (and Auditability — the framework's own OTAA invariant)
- **Where:** `docs/agentic-primitives.md:1409` (DELEGATE row: "no — unless the receiving agent's policy treats acceptance as a Domain Outcome…"); `:1410` (ERROR row: "no — unless the recovery path is `degrade` and the degradation is itself a policy decision")
- **What the framework claims:** GDR emission is conditional on the *downstream* policy interpretation of the signal, not the issuer's state.
- **What's wrong:** This violates the OTAA "Auditable" invariant in the local sense: an auditor reconstructing what happened from the issuing ring's emissions cannot determine whether a GDR was created elsewhere. To know, they must traverse the receiver's policy state *at the time of delegation/degradation* — coupling that is acknowledged nowhere in the spec. This also creates a side-channel: a curious caller can probe receivers for which delegations or degradations produce GDRs, leaking information about boundary policy.
- **Recommended fix:** Require the issuer to emit a `delegation_attempted` / `degradation_proposed` observability event with a deterministic `gdr_required: true | false | conditional-on-receiver` field set from local policy at issuance, and require the receiver to emit a GDR with `triggered_by: <issuer_event_id>` whenever it does emit one. This preserves issuer-side audit reconstructability and makes the cross-boundary handoff observable.

### O47-F04 — No specified intersection between Composition Pattern and Maturity Level

- **ID:** O47-F04
- **Title:** Composition Patterns and Maturity Levels are claimed orthogonal but the cross-axis matrix is absent
- **Severity:** Medium
- **Confidence:** Established — primer claims orthogonality; no doc supplies the intersection
- **Dimension:** Actionability (and Coherence)
- **Where:** `docs/shared-vocabulary.md:58–62` (L1–L5 definitions) vs. `:287–296` (4 Composition Patterns); primer declares both first-class. No Pattern × Maturity table in `agentic-primitives.md`, `agentic-governance-framework.md`, or the GRC profile.
- **What the framework claims:** L2 = "Ring 0 + Ring 1 operational for **high-risk agents**" (`shared-vocabulary.md:59`) — i.e., Validation Pipeline applied to a risk subset.
- **What's wrong:** The risk-tiered phrasing of L2 is the only place the two axes touch, and only for one tier. The framework does not specify (a) what Pattern is required for Tier-2 vs Tier-3 agents at L3, (b) whether L4 requires Full Governed or whether it can be reached by GDF + Ring 3 sentinels, (c) how an organization with a mixed portfolio (some MVC, some Full Governed) computes a single Maturity level. An assessor cannot conformance-check without inventing the table.
- **Recommended fix:** Add a normative Pattern × Maturity × Risk-Tier matrix to `agentic-governance-framework.md` § Framework Maturity Model. Even a 5×4×4 table with a few cells marked "not required" or "out of scope" would close this. If the answer is "this is left to the organization," say so — that is itself an architectural commitment that should be marked Open.

### O47-F05 — Multi-Agent governance recursion has no trust anchor (only a depth cap)

- **ID:** O47-F05
- **Title:** Governance Collapse Rule terminates recursion by recommendation, not by a specified trust anchor
- **Severity:** Medium
- **Confidence:** Established — direct comparison of two recursion-termination patterns
- **Dimension:** Coherence (and Defensibility)
- **Where:** `docs/agentic-primitives.md:746–755` (Recursive Governance Problem for environments — explicit "trust anchor: human-authored root configuration") vs. `:1325–1332` (Governance Collapse Rule for multi-agent — "max nesting depth 3 … flatten by broadening scope")
- **What the framework claims:** Recursion terminates because depth is capped at 3 and beyond that "systems should flatten."
- **What's wrong:** The environment-recursion section solves the same recursion problem with a *substantive* termination (root config is signed, manually reviewed, the trust anchor) not a depth cap. The Multi-Agent section uses only a depth cap. A 3-level orchestrator hierarchy where every level is itself agentic still has no specified trust anchor — there is no rule saying "the outermost Ring 2's policy must be human-authored and version-controlled in the same way the environment root config is." The asymmetry is not justified.
- **Recommended fix:** Either explicitly extend the environment-recursion trust-anchor pattern to multi-agent governance (the outermost Ring 2's policy is the trust anchor; must be human-authored; signed; reviewed) or document why the two recursions are dis-analogous. The first is cheaper and more honest.

### O47-F06 — Structural claims in canonical docs are unmarked; the confidence gradient is applied to *content* claims, not to AGF's own *architecture* claims

- **ID:** O47-F06
- **Title:** AGF's confidence gradient is not applied to load-bearing architectural assertions
- **Severity:** Medium
- **Confidence:** Established — by inspection of `shared-vocabulary.md` and the Multi-Agent / Composability sections
- **Dimension:** Defensibility (and Credibility — dogfood discipline)
- **Where:** `docs/shared-vocabulary.md` whole document (no `<Confidence>` markers); `docs/agentic-primitives.md:1327` ("the outermost Ring 2 is authoritative on scope conflicts" — declarative); `:1332` ("recommends a maximum of 3 governance levels"); `:1391` ("max_delegation_depth: 3 (recommended)"); `:339` (Trust transition GDR field set — declarative)
- **What the framework claims:** Per `intent.md:28`, claims should carry confidence. Per F27 closeout, the discipline applies to "load-bearing empirical and novel claims."
- **What's wrong:** Several of the framework's *structural* choices are exactly that — load-bearing and novel — yet are presented as flat declaratives. "Max 3 governance levels," "outermost Ring 2 wins," "trust degrades faster than it builds," "DEFER is a resolved state," "max_delegation_depth: 3" are normative architectural decisions, several of which would land as Informed under AGF's own gradient (single-source synthesis, plausible, untested at scale). Leaving them unmarked treats the architecture as more settled than the content; this is the inverse of what the dogfood promise implies.
- **Recommended fix:** Apply `<Confidence level="informed">` (or block-form equivalent) to the structural claims listed above and to the Composability Interface signal restrictions, the Governance Collapse Rule, the trust ladder asymmetry, and the per-signal emission rules. Where a claim is genuinely Established (e.g., "policy as code is more auditable than prose policy") leave it; where it's a synthesis decision, label it.

### O47-F07 — Trust-Ladder demotion against a mandatory gate produces no specified audit artifact

- **ID:** O47-F07
- **Title:** "Rejected at the policy layer before emission" leaves the rejected attempt unobserved
- **Severity:** Low
- **Confidence:** Informed — the spec says "rejected"; whether anything is logged is not stated
- **Dimension:** Mechanical Integrity (and Auditability)
- **Where:** `docs/agentic-primitives.md:348` ("the GDR for any such attempted demotion against a mandatory gate would be rejected at the policy layer before emission")
- **What the framework claims:** Demotion attempts that would affect mandatory gates are rejected before GDR emission.
- **What's wrong:** "Rejected before emission" leaves no audit trail of the attempt. If sentinels can fire spuriously or be adversarially triggered, the operator can't see the attempt rate. An adversary probing which gates are mandatory can infer the answer from whether their trigger produced a GDR. Both are addressable.
- **Recommended fix:** Require an observability event (`trust_demotion_rejected`, with rejection reason and the policy that blocked it) at the policy layer, distinct from a GDR. Keep the no-GDR rule for the demotion itself; add the no-silent-failure rule for the attempt.

## Pattern observations (cross-finding meta)

**M-P1: The framework specifies axes confidently and intersections vaguely.** F01, F04, and (in part) F03 share a shape — each axis (Patterns, Maturity, Modes, Rings, Security Levels) is well-named and well-defined; the cells where two or more axes meet are either underspecified, polymorphic, or missing. A multi-axis conformance check is impossible from the docs as written. This is the seam most likely to tear under implementation.

**M-P2: Lifecycle and signal docs are written by different lobes.** F02 and F03 are both inter-doc inconsistencies between `agentic-primitives.md` (the signal-emission promises) and `governance-decision-record.md` (the lifecycle reality). The per-signal table promises behaviors the GDR lifecycle table does not accommodate. This is a coherence regression introduced by the very D5/D8/D9 unification that closed the prior overloading findings — the unifying tables now over-promise relative to the schema.

**M-P3: Recursion is solved unevenly across self-similar problems.** F05 and the broader pattern: environment-recursion gets a substantive trust anchor, multi-agent recursion gets a depth cap, harness-self-governance (D7 — the harness governs the agent; the framework specifies the harness; #18 verifies the harness configuration; what verifies #18?) is not addressed at all. Three instances of the same problem, three different (or absent) terminations.

**M-P4: Dogfooding the gradient stops at the architecture boundary.** F06 is the meta. AGF applies its own confidence gradient to claims about external evidence and to novelty claims about competing frameworks. It does not apply the gradient to its own structural decisions. This makes the architecture appear more settled than the framework itself believes it to be; in turn, the unannotated structural claims propagate into profile docs as if they were Established.

## Bottom-line verdict

I would cite AGF in the same scope as the Codex 7/10 verdict — for **architecture framing, gate semantics, and governance-pattern design** — and add one specific scope: I would cite the GDR schema and per-signal emission discipline as the most defensible original synthesis. I would **not** cite AGF as a multi-axis conformance specification (Pattern × Maturity × Mode × Risk-Tier) because that matrix doesn't exist yet, and I would footnote the F02/F03 inconsistencies if I were citing the per-signal table.

The single change that would move my score most is **F04 — publish the Pattern × Maturity × Risk-Tier matrix.** It's the cell most readers will need first, it doesn't require external evidence, and shipping it would force F02/F03/F06 (mechanical and dogfooding gaps) to surface in the cells where they bite. F33 (adopter case study) would unlock Public Readiness; F04 would unlock Actionability and Coherence simultaneously. Of the two, F04 is internally addressable; F33 is not.

The Codex 7/10 Public Readiness plateau **holds under structural read, and is mildly generous on Coherence.** It correctly identifies that adoption proof is the binding constraint. It under-weights that even editorially-tractable structural seams remain — F01 (a vocabulary collision the D10 sweep should have caught), F02 (a lifecycle gap introduced by the unifying tables), and F04 (the missing cross-axis matrix) are all closeable inside one editorial sprint and would each move the score. The framework reads as architecturally honest where the gradient is applied; the unannotated structural claims (F06) are the credibility ceiling Codex hasn't been pushing on, because Codex is reading for evidence and tone, not for whether the architecture's own commitments carry the markers AGF demands of others.
